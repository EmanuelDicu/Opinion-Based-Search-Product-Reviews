Introduction
The “Opinion-Based Search for Product Reviews (Smartphones)” project aims to develop an intelligent product review analysis and search system based on identifying and interpreting user opinions on specific aspects of a product, not just the overall polarity of the review. Through this system, users will be able to explore detailed information about concrete features such as camera quality, battery life, processor performance, design or screen clarity. Instead of returning just a positive or negative label, the system will organize and synthesize the opinions expressed on each feature, providing a granular picture of collective perceptions. The ultimate goal is to transform review analysis from a subjective and difficult process into a structured semantic exploration, in which the user can formulate queries such as “What do users say about the battery life of the iPhone 15?” or “How is the camera of the Samsung Galaxy S23 compared to that of the Pixel 8?” and receive results EXPRESS and relevant .

Context and similar works
Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) is one of the most active research directions in natural language processing. The first significant works were carried out by Hu and Liu (2004), who proposed the automatic extraction of product features through syntactic rules and the identification of adjectives associated with them. These contributions defined the basic structure of aspect-based analysis, forming the foundation of modern approaches. Later, Eirinaki et al. (2012) introduced the concept of feature-based opinion mining and ranking, in which each review is indexed according to the identified aspects and the associated sentiment, providing the possibility to compare similar products according to the satisfaction reported for various features.
In recent years, the development of transformer neural models, such as BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa or T5, has revolutionized the field, allowing the learning of contextual representations of words and the simultaneous extraction of pairs (aspect, opinion). In the recent literature (2023–2025), joint aspect-opinion extraction models have been noted, which treat the identification of aspects and opinions as a unified task, using token-level classification. At the same time, T5 and BART models are used for automatic aspect-based summarization, highlighting the main opinions about a certain feature. These developments confirm the technical feasibility and relevance of the proposed project theme.
Data sets used
To train and test the system, the project uses the “Amazon Reviews 2023” dataset, created by McAuley Lab and published on the Hugging Face platform (https://huggingface.co/datasets/McAuley-Lab/Amazon-Reviews-2023). This is one of the largest publicly available corpora, containing over 200 million reviews from multiple product categories. The data is well-structured and includes essential metadata such as product identifier, review text, review score, publication date, and product category.
Of all the available categories, the project will focus on the subset “Cell_Phones_and_Accessories”, which contains reviews of mobile phones and related accessories. This choice is motivated by the fact that smartphones are complex products, with numerous distinct features that can be analyzed independently: camera, battery, screen, processor, sound, design, etc. At the same time, this category has a large volume of detailed reviews, providing a solid basis for automatic identification of aspects and evaluation of associated sentiments.
Using such a diverse set also offers an additional advantage: the possibility of extending the system to other product areas without major architectural changes. By preserving the structural consistency of the data, the system will be able to generalize the analysis and search process opinions at the inter-categorical level .

General system architecture
The system architecture is designed modularly, being divided into distinct but interconnected stages: data preprocessing, automatic extraction of aspects and opinions, indexing in the search engine and processing of user queries. The implementation will be done in Python, using established libraries for natural language processing (spaCy, Transformers) and efficient storage (Elasticsearch).
The preprocessing stage involves cleaning the texts, removing special characters, HTML tags and irrelevant content, followed by tokenization and lemmatization. In the next stage, the extraction of features and corresponding opinions will be performed through a combination of methods: rule-based syntactic analysis and pre-trained neural models for ABSA.
The models developed by Yangheng and available on Hugging Face, such as "yangheng/deberta-v3-base-end2end-absa" and "yangheng/deberta-v3-base-absa-v1.1", will be used, which allow the simultaneous detection of aspects, opinions and their polarity. The results will be structured as tuples (product_id, feature, opinion, review_text, sentiment), which will then be indexed in an Elasticsearch database.
The search engine will allow both classic, keyword-based queries (BM25 score) and semantic searches based on embedding similarity. This combination ensures a balance between precision and coverage, giving the user the ability to obtain relevant answers even for question complex .

Originality of the project and inference of new features
A major originality of the project is the introduction of a feature inference mechanism based on existing knowledge. The system will not be limited to explicit aspects extracted from reviews, but will be able to infer emergent concepts generated by semantically combining existing features.
For example, if the system already has information about aspects such as “processor”, “performance” and “fast response”, it will also be able to infer a composite feature such as “gaming experience”, even if this term does not appear explicitly in the dataset. This way, users will be able to formulate free-form queries – such as “How does the phone perform in games?” – and the system will automatically identify relevant features from the knowledge base and provide coherent answers, based on existing evidence.
This approach transforms the system from a simple search engine into a tool capable of semantic reasoning and generalization. The mechanism is based on the vector similarity between the embeddings of aspects and opinions, but also on the frequency of co-occurrences in reviews. In the long term, the inference component will allow the system to evolve dynamically, gradually adding new concepts and strengthening the relationships between them, thus creating a semantic base broad and adaptive .

Possible problems and observations
Implementing such a system involves several challenges. A first difficulty lies in linguistic ambiguity – certain words can have multiple meanings, and their interpretation depends on the context. Terms such as “display” or “screen” can refer to both the physical part and the software interface. Reviews can also contain ambiguous expressions or mixed opinions, in which the same text expresses both appreciation and criticism.
Another important issue is multilingualism: reviews may be written in different languages or may contain hybrid terms. The use of multilingual models (such as mBERT or XLM-R) can mitigate this difficulty, but does not eliminate it completely. From a technical point of view, indexing a large volume of data requires optimizations and caching mechanisms for frequent queries, and performance evaluation must be performed on a manually labeled dataset to measure the accuracy of the extraction. and relevance results .

Updated implementation (recent changes)
The current implementation has been updated with the following changes:

ETL pipeline: Stricter data quality and validation. A ReviewValidator applies configurable filters (minimum review length, maximum caps ratio, spam patterns, rating sanity, duplicate detection, content quality). The dataset loader reports detailed statistics on rejected reviews. This reduces noise and improves downstream aspect extraction and indexing.
Feature inference: High-level features are now defined with weighted aspects. Each aspect has a positive weight and a negative weight (e.g. pos_weight, neg_weight) so that sentiment correlation can differ for positive vs negative mentions. The FeatureInferenceEngine has been simplified: it computes pos_score and neg_score from these weights, then derives overall sentiment and confidence from the weighted scores. The previous LLM-based inference option was removed in favor of this faster, rule-based weighted logic.
LLM summarization: Backend summarization no longer uses OpenAI. It uses Ollama with the qwen3:8b model. The maximum number of reviews sent to the LLM for summarization is configurable (LLM_SUMMARY_MAX_REVIEWS). The LLM service uses ollama.chat() and falls back gracefully if Ollama is unavailable.
Embeddings and Ollama: Dense embeddings use the qwen3-embedding:4b model via Ollama (embedding_service). If Ollama is unreachable at startup, the embedding service sets an internal “unavailable” flag so that embedding generation is skipped without repeated connection-error messages.
Dependencies and fixes: Backend requirements use the ollama package instead of openai/httpx. A typo in the embedding service (wrong key for the embedding response) was fixed so that model verification and dimension detection work correctly.

conclusions
By combining modern natural language processing techniques, advanced neural models and information search mechanisms, the "Opinion-Based Search for Product Reviews (Smartphones)" project proposes an innovative approach to review analysis. The system will allow for a more detailed and nuanced understanding of consumer opinions, facilitating informed decision-making in the purchasing process.
In addition, the semantic inference component of the new features adds an additional layer of intelligence, allowing the system to respond to complex queries and adapt to the natural language of users. In the long term, this architecture could be extended to other product categories or even other domains, providing a general infrastructure for opinion-based search and automatic knowledge extraction from unstructured texts.
